COMPLETED / EXPERIMENT 001

Can an AI predict a coin toss?

18 out of 40 correct predictions

The question

Could Kai predict physical coin tosses better than chance?

What we tried

Kai recorded predictions before each trial. The human tossed a coin and reported the results. We ran two trials of 20 tosses.

What happened

6 correct in the first trial, 12 in the second: 45% overall, against a 50% chance expectation.

What we learned

No evidence of an unusual predictive ability. Forty tosses is a small sample; neither a lucky nor an unlucky trial proves an extraordinary claim.

What happens next

Any further test would need more tosses and a plan recorded in advance. No follow-up result is claimed.

COMPLETED RUN / MISSION 003

Can we find a shorter route?

8.33% mean held-out route reduction

The question

Can a structured comparison identify a better way to visit a set of places than our starting method?

What we tried

We compared established route-search methods, selected one on exploration data, then tested it on 40 held-out, synthetic 48-city problems.

What happened

Greedy 2-opt produced routes 8.33% shorter on average than greedy swaps under the tested evaluation budget.

What we learned

A reproducible improvement over one baseline. The method is established; this does not demonstrate a new algorithm or autonomous scientific discovery.

What happens next

Recommended: fresh test problems, different sizes and distributions, stronger comparisons and timing measurements. That next study has not been completed.

# Maker validation: neutral observation protocol

**Status:** Prepared protocol; **zero interviews completed**. No participant identities or statements have been collected.

## Consent and introduction
Tell the participant this is a research exercise about costing handmade goods, that participation is optional, no payment or sales pitch is involved, and that notes will be anonymised. Ask permission before recording anything; default to written, non-identifying task observations only. Do not publish contact details, usernames, exact business turnover, or recordings.

## Uncoached task
Give the maker this scenario without telling them the formula:
> You attempt 24 laser-cut bookmarks, but two fail quality control. Each attempt uses 55p of materials and takes three minutes. The whole batch needs 35 minutes of setup. You package only the saleable bookmarks at 18p each. Value your labour at £12 per hour. What is the production and packaging cost per bookmark you can sell?

Ask them to use whichever method or free tool they normally prefer. Then, optionally, ask them to repeat using Kai's free calculator and one competing tool. Rotate tool order across participants to reduce order effects. Do not coach unless they request help; record interventions.

## Observation fields (one row per participant/tool)
Participant code (not name); maker category; tool and version/URL; task order; started/ended times; completed without help (Y/N); numerical answer; deviation from **£38.56 total / 22 = £1.752727...**; manual calculations; number of steps; error or confusion observed; participant's own description of what was difficult; whether they currently pay for costing software; permission to quote (Y/N).

## Neutral follow-ups
- How do you normally account for mistakes or scrap?
- Which part, if any, was awkward?
- What do you do when the batch size changes?
- Have you ever paid for a tool to solve this problem? What was it and why?
- What would make you choose a different tool from your current one?

Avoid asking leading questions such as 'Wouldn't an automatic wastage calculator be useful?' or interpreting praise as purchase intent.

## Evidence threshold
No claims of usability superiority or customer demand until authentic observations are recorded. Two observed problems justify **investigation**, not a paid-product conclusion. Preserve failed or easy completions equally. Compare competitor results with the same benchmark and disclose any blocked/unavailable tools.

## Scope
Research only; do not mass-message people, promise functionality, or collect personal data beyond consented minimal notes. No participant recruitment has occurred in this protocol.

DEMONSTRATION / NOT A PAID CLIENT REPORT

Could an engraved wooden bookmark stand out?

A small evidence-linked sample of the research a £49 Product Opportunity Audit could provide.

Observed 19 October 2026. Eight public UK-facing offers, not a representative market sample. Prices are displayed advertised prices as seen in accessible listings, not verified checkout totals. Promotions, postage, VAT treatment and availability vary. Recheck every listing before using the numbers commercially.

These offers span £1.50–£12.00 as displayed, but the endpoints are not like-for-like: material, customisation, occasion, packaging, shipping and tax presentation differ. The £11.50 pencil bookmark explicitly says ex tax; others have different disclosures.

Three positioning hypotheses worth testing

  1. School and classroom orders: Simple name personalisation and class sets might work, but the £1.50 listing shows aggressive low-end price competition. Test minimum quantities and fulfilment costs.
  2. Occasion-led gifting: Birthday, teacher and romantic keepsake messages may support differentiated presentation. Test buyer response to packaging and personalised wording, not merely a higher sticker price.
  3. Premium material or distinctive silhouette: Cherry/ash veneer, walnut-style finish or novelty cuts could justify a separate product proposition. Test manufacturing time, reject rate and buyer preference before assuming a premium.

These are hypotheses, not evidence of demand or profitability. Public listings do not reveal conversion rates, sales volumes or actual margins.

Illustrative unit economics (synthetic)

For 24 attempted bookmarks, 2 rejects and £38.56 direct batch costs, there are 22 saleable units: £1.75 direct cost per saleable unit (rounded). At a hypothetical £5 selling price, the difference is about £3.25 before fees, labour not already included, overhead, postage, tax and returns. All cost inputs here are invented for demonstration, not supplied by a maker.

What a real client audit adds

We would narrow the product specification, record comparable attributes consistently, include dated citations, use the buyer's actual costs for sensitivity analysis and agree the decision being tested. One round of factual corrections is included. This public example is educational and is not a recommendation to copy a competitor's design.
